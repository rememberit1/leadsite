Plumbing issues common in Kensington homes

  • Older homes in the Kensington Historic District often have a mix of original cast-iron drains, older galvanized supply branches and updated copper or PEX, so recurring slow drains or low upstairs water pressure are common.
  • Mid-century homes in Parkwood, Rock Creek Hills and Kensington Heights sit under a dense canopy of mature oaks and maples; root intrusion at clay or cast-iron sewer joints is a top cause of main-line backups.
  • Many basements along the Rock Creek corridor rely on sump pumps during summer cloudbursts; test the float switch each spring and consider a battery backup.
  • All underground sewer work and gas water heater replacements in Kensington require a WSSC Water permit and a WSSC-licensed master plumber or gasfitter.

Frequently asked questions

Do plumbers in Kensington need to be WSSC-registered?

Yes. Kensington is in WSSC Water's service area, and plumbers and sewer drain cleaners must be WSSC Water-registered. Ask for the registration number.

Who do I call for a sewer backup in Kensington?

Call WSSC Water's 24-hour emergency line (301-206-4002) and a registered plumber. The plumber determines whether the blockage is on your side or WSSC's side.

What should I do first in a plumbing emergency in Kensington?

Shut off the main water valve for a burst pipe or major leak, and stop running water if sewage is backing up. Then call a plumber. Our emergency plumbing checklist covers the first 10 minutes.

Do I need a permit for a water heater or sewer line repair in Kensington?

Replacing a gas water heater ($180 WSSC Short Form permit) or repairing an underground water or sewer line requires a WSSC Water permit and inspection. Like-for-like electric water heater swaps are usually exempt. See our water heater permit guide and sewer line repair guide.
