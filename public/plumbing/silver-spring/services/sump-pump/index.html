Sump Pump Repair & Installation in Silver Spring, MD

Quick answer: If your basement floods during heavy summer storms, check that the sump pump float moves freely and the pump runs when you pour water into the pit. A battery backup keeps it running during power outages, which often come with the same storms. If sewage, not groundwater, comes up the floor drain, the problem is a sewer backup, not the sump pump.

Common failures

  • Stuck float switch, burned-out motor, or a frozen or clogged discharge line.
  • Pump too small for the pit inflow during heavy storms.
  • No backup during power outages.

What it costs

  • Published 2026 Silver Spring estimates for a submersible sump pump installed in an existing pit mostly land around $1,100–$1,300 (homeyou typical range $864–$1,213; NailThePrice average about $1,280; ProMatcher $1,300–$1,600).
  • Costs go up for a new pit cut into a concrete floor, a long or buried discharge line, a new electrical outlet, and a battery backup. Ask for an itemized written quote that names the pump model and horsepower.

Groundwater vs. sewer water

Clear water at the walls or floor joints is groundwater, a sump and drainage issue. Dirty water from the floor drain is a sewer backup. See our guide to who pays for a sewer backup.

Frequently asked questions

How often should a sump pump be replaced?

Many pumps last around 7–10 years depending on use. Test it every spring and before storm season.

How much does a sump pump cost in Silver Spring?

Published 2026 Silver Spring estimates cluster around $1,100–$1,300 installed for a submersible pump in an existing pit (homeyou: $864–$1,213 typical; NailThePrice: about $1,280 average; ProMatcher: $1,300–$1,600). Swapping a pump in an existing pit is cheaper. Digging a new pit through a concrete floor, a long buried discharge line, a new outlet, or a battery backup each add to the bill. Get a written, itemized quote.

Is a battery backup worth it?

Usually, yes, if your basement has flooded before. The storms that fill the pit are often the same storms that knock out power, and a primary pump does nothing without electricity. A battery backup or a water-powered backup keeps water moving during an outage.

Sump pump or backwater valve: which do I need?

They solve different problems. A sump pump removes groundwater that seeps in from under the slab or along the walls (usually clear water). A backwater valve stops sewage from flowing back into the house through a floor drain when the sewer main surcharges (dirty water). If you've had both, you may need both. A backwater valve is plumbing work on the building drain, so use a WSSC Water-registered plumber.
