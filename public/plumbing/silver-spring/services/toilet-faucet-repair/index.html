Toilet & Faucet Repair in Silver Spring, MD

Quick answer: A running toilet is usually a worn flapper or a fill valve, and both are cheap parts. A dripping faucet usually needs a cartridge or washer. Call a plumber if the toilet rocks or leaks at the base (the wax seal or flange), or if the shutoff valves under it are old and won't turn.

Call (240) 226-3502

Quick fixes

  • Flapper replacement for 'phantom flushes'.
  • Adjust the float so water stops below the overflow tube.
  • Replace the faucet cartridge (bring the old one to the store to match it).

When to call

  • Water on the floor at the toilet base: the seal may be rotting the subfloor.
  • Corroded or seized shutoff valves (common in older homes). Replacing them prevents a flood the next time someone turns them.

Frequently asked questions

Is a running toilet really a big deal?

Yes. WSSC Water says toilet leaks are one of the leading causes of high bills, and estimates a small toilet leak (about 200 gallons a day) adds nearly $475 to a quarterly bill at FY27 rates.

Will WSSC lower my bill if a toilet was leaking?

Toilet leaks don't have their own adjustment. If the bill was at least three times your usage for the same period a year earlier, you can ask for WSSC's high-bill adjustment, which removes 50% of the excess once usage is back to normal. See our WSSC high bill guide.
